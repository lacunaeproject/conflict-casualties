    Why ceasefire months aren't zero

    Monthly figures are the change in the Ministry of Health's cumulative total. During truces that change includes new killings, bodies recovered from rubble, and earlier deaths confirmed after review, so the lighter bars overstate new violence. See the breakdown below.

    Read more

    The official Gaza toll has risen by roughly 6,700 since the ceasefire. Only about a fifth of that is new killing. The rest are people killed before the ceasefire who are only now being counted: bodies pulled from the rubble, and deaths added to the record after the Ministry of Health's committee verified them. Those additions arrive in weekly batches, so the steps they create in the cumulative chart above are not new surges in violence.

    Under the ceasefire, Israeli forces were to hold 53% of the Strip behind the so-called "Yellow Line." By March 2026 they held about 64%, and on May 28, 2026, Prime Minister Netanyahu ordered the army to take 70%. The last Israeli hostage held in Gaza, the body of Ran Gvili, was returned on January 26, 2026. None remain in Gaza.

    Historical context · Al-Nakba

    This war did not begin on October 7, 2023.

    It is the most recent chapter of a dispossession that began in 1948 — al-Nakba, “the catastrophe.” To read the figures above without that history is to read them incomplete.

    Read the history

    Between the UN Partition Plan of November 1947 and the armistice of 1949, Zionist militias and the newly-formed Israeli military carried out a coordinated campaign — documented as Plan Dalet — that emptied more than half of Mandatory Palestine's Arab population. Villages were razed. Survivors were barred from returning under Israeli law. Their land, homes, and bank accounts were absorbed into the new state.

    What began in 1948 never ended. The 1967 occupation extended it into the West Bank and Gaza. Decades of settlement expansion, home demolitions, and displacement in East Jerusalem, the Naqab, and the Jordan Valley continued it. Gaza has been under blockade since 2007. Palestinian scholars and UN experts increasingly describe the events since October 2023 as a second Nakba — this time televised, this time with more than 73,000 dead.

    Notes & caveats

    • No figure is complete. Gaza MoH itself states thousands of dead remain under rubble. Peer-reviewed research published in The Lancet Global Health estimated 75,200 violent deaths through January 2025 — roughly 35% higher than the MoH count for the same period — and did not capture deaths from starvation, disease, or collapsed healthcare. As of September 24, 2026, Gaza MoH reports 73,928 killed — including 1,400+ since the October 2025 ceasefire — and Wikipedia's conflict tracker cites 75,131+ Palestinians killed across Gaza and the West Bank; excess non-violent deaths likely push the true toll substantially higher.
    All notes and sources
    • Some category counts are no longer updated. MoH's children (20,179) and women (12,500) breakdowns, medical personnel (1,701), and journalists (262) have not changed in months; starvation deaths (463) and aid-seekers killed (2,615) were last reported in mid-2026. The Committee to Protect Journalists, after a September 2026 review, counts 191 journalists killed, using a narrower definition.
    • The age and sex breakdown comes from the Ministry of Health's list of dead identified by name, which trails the overall total.
    • OCHA relies on MoH for Gaza but independently verifies West Bank incidents. Its counts include fatalities from both Israeli military and settler attacks.
    • B'Tselem publishes a conservative, named-only database of verified individuals. Its totals trail MoH but document each death in detail.
    • Israeli soldier deaths are taken from IDF press releases and reporting by The Times of Israel. The count shown is soldiers killed in Gaza ground operations since October 27, 2023.
    • The governorate breakdown shown is a model, not a reported statistic. It distributes the overall Gaza total by pre-war population weighted for reported damage — directional only.
    • The dashboard is rebuilt from the Tech for Palestine API (which ingests OCHA flash updates and MoH briefings). Re-running the build pulls the latest daily data.

Visual record

Homes on both sides of the fence

Numbers hold death at a distance. These photographs do not. They show what was destroyed: first in the Israeli communities along the Gaza border on October 7, 2023, then across the Gaza Strip in the war that followed. The people who lived in these homes, on either side, were not the ones who chose this violence. Again and again, decisions made by armed groups and governments have fallen hardest on civilians.

Israel · October 7, 2023

The attack on the border communities.

On October 7, 2023, gunmen led by Hamas broke through the Gaza border fence and attacked kibbutzim, towns, army posts, and a music festival in southern Israel. About 1,200 people were killed, most of them civilians, and 251 were taken hostage into Gaza. In Be’eri, 101 civilians and 31 security personnel were killed and 125 homes damaged or destroyed. In Kfar Aza, at least 62 residents were killed. In Nir Oz, 47 people were killed and 76 taken hostage, about a quarter of the community. Many homes were set on fire with families sheltering inside.

The people who lived in these homes, on either side, were not the ones who chose this violence.

Gaza · October 2023 – 2025

The destruction of Gaza.

Israel’s military campaign after October 7 damaged or destroyed between 60% and 70% of all structures in the Gaza Strip, according to satellite analysis by the UN and Yale’s Conflict Observatory. Entire neighborhoods of Gaza City, Khan Younis, Jabalia, and Rafah were reduced to rubble. Hospitals, schools, bakeries, universities, and refugee camps were among the structures hit. The final UN–EU–World Bank damage assessment, published in April 2026, counted 371,888 housing units destroyed or damaged and put the cost of reconstruction at $71.4 billion over the next decade.

About these photographs

Every image on this page is hosted on Wikimedia Commons under a Creative Commons or public-domain license. Originals are available at the credit link under each photograph. Many of the Gaza photographs were taken by Palestinian photographers working for APA images or local outlets; others are official UNRWA documentation of destroyed facilities. The photographs from Israel come from PikiWiki (the Israeli free image collection), the Israel Government Press Office, and independent contributors.

This gallery shows physical destruction on both sides, not the bodies of the dead. The choice to exclude the latter is not a judgment about its importance — those images are widely available and bear witness in a way photographs of buildings cannot — but a choice about what this page is for. For the broader visual record, the UN OCHA oPt, UNRWA, Al Jazeera, and the Yale Conflict Observatory maintain ongoing documentation.

Quantitative estimates of the destruction cited on this page come from the UN Satellite Centre (UNOSAT) Gaza damage assessments, the Yale Conflict Observatory, and the Gaza Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment (UN, EU & World Bank, April 2026). Figures for the October 7 attacks are drawn from Israeli official and press reporting as summarized by Wikipedia.
